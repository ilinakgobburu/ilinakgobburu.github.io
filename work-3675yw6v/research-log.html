Research log: ideas tested and rejected

About fifteen trading ideas tested in October and November 2026. For most, the question, the rules and a pass bar were written down before the run, and the result was recorded as it came out; a few were descriptive or ran on data already pulled, and say so. Most failed. They're listed here with their bars, because a list of only the winners would hide how they were found.

How the tests were kept honest

  • Pass bars fixed in advance, with any later change dated and explained.
  • One-shot final tests, and gates that stop a weak idea before it can spend one.
  • Chance baselines: random stocks, permutation and rotation tests.
  • Mid and stress fills for the option trades; a fixed cost for the stock books.
  • Tested code, and independent reviews before the later runs; after-the-fact results are labeled as such.

Option selling: the wheel

  • Timing the wheel with RSI. Skip puts when the stock looked stretched, or calls when it looked weak. Bar, set after the class results were seen: beat the plain wheel in 7 of 9 windows. Result: 2, 0 and 2 of 9; stretched stocks kept going.
  • Pairs-traded wheels, four versions. Bar: beat the two plain wheels combined in 4 of 5 years. Result: 0 of 5 each; in both inverse versions the short Nvidia leg ran away and was margin-called.
  • Sell only when implied beats realized vol. Bar: 5 of 9 windows, a weak bar. Result: passed on its second, adjusted version, then held 5 of 9 (6 at stress fills) on the test windows, mostly by sitting out the 2022 sell-off. It skipped nearly half the puts. A weak pass, not adopted.
  • The pair of wheels whose losses coincided least. Result: it ranked below the median pair afterward, and the ranking didn't persist (p 0.30 and 0.21).
  • A barbell: wheel the mean-reverting stock, hold the momentum stock. Bar: beat the alternatives in 6 of 8 folds. Result: 3, 2 and 5 of 8.
  • Practitioner management: early closes, rolls and a trend filter. Result (no pass count was set): beat the plain wheel's Sharpe ratio in only 7 and 8 of 18 stock-windows.
  • Selling puts when market-wide option premium is high. Result: better on 4 of 5 stocks, but its registered rotation test gave p 0.31 against a bar of 0.10: one crash entry carried the whole difference.
  • A jet-fuel hedge for an airline. Result: Delta and fuel moved together, so the hedge had nothing to offset; it never beat the plain wheel.

The Poor Man's Covered Call

  • Seasonality screens for the stock choice. Two screens (Apple, then Delta) passed their rules (p 0.023 and 0.020), but every peak month contained earnings, so both were earnings effects rather than seasonality. Apple's pattern held in later years; Delta's didn't.
  • The best in-sample option setting. Result: it lost to the baseline on all four cost bases in both out-of-sample samples.
  • Hedging the direction out. Result: the screen's pick and two follow-ups all failed to beat T-bills on all four cost bases. Full write-up →

Volatility and machine learning

  • Can ML pick the most overpriced options? The pre-chosen logistic regression trailed a one-line ratio rule by 0.42 vol points and failed its bar; the simple rule it had to beat scored +1.03 vol points on held-out years (t 4.1). Full write-up →
  • Can you time selling single-stock volatility? "Options look rich against last month" had no relation to the next month's premium over 120 months (r = −0.01). Full write-up →
  • A screen for options that look too rich. A one-day snapshot; the top of the list was mostly special situations (pending deals, new listings, earnings timing), not mispricing.

Momentum, reversal and data

  • Switching between reversal and momentum, three published signals. Bar: t ≥ 1 and a Sharpe ratio above a fixed blend. Result: the best had t 0.52, so the gate failed and the final-test window was left unused; its whole edge was one month, April 2020.
  • Data traps caught before any backtest: a stock split that showed a false 74% loss on a long option, and missing bids returned in a type the code had to read as zero bids.
  • A survivor-only universe: none of nine departed firms checked had implied vol in the vendor's data, so an ML panel built on it would hold only survivors.
