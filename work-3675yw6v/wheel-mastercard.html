The wheel on Mastercard, then five stocks

The wheel sells cash-secured puts, takes the shares if a put is assigned, then sells calls against them until they are called away. It is short volatility: like selling insurance, it is paid a premium up front, and it should profit when implied volatility is above what the stock then realizes. The thesis was a higher Sharpe ratio than holding Mastercard; part two runs the same wheel on five stocks as one portfolio.

How it was tested

  • Survivorship-bias-free stock screen. Mastercard came out of a mean-reversion screen of the S&P 500 as it stood at the end of 2023, including stocks later removed.
  • Settings fixed before testing. Chosen on 2024–2025 and locked in before 2026 was run, once. A walk-forward test checked the choice.
  • Black-Scholes valuation. Implied volatility and delta for each option come from its quoted price.
  • Cash account under Reg T. Every put is cash-secured and every call covered; T-bill interest and dividends are counted.
  • Fill-price check against actual trades, plus a stress test selling at the bid.

Mastercard: about the same return, far less risk

  • Where the profit came from. Option premium was 60% of it and T-bill interest on the cash 29%; the one assignment supplied the other 11%.
  • Robust to worse fills. Selling every option at the bid cut the 2026 return from +10.1% to +9.4%.

Part two: the same wheel on five stocks

The wheel's real risk is one stock falling hard while it holds the shares: Fiserv's wheel returned −48.7% in 2024–2025. So the same wheel ran on all five stocks the screen picked (Mastercard, APA, Visa, Fiserv, Analog Devices), $100,000 each, as one $500,000 portfolio, against the five stocks held, T-bills and the Cboe PutWrite index.

What it shows
  • Spreading it out cut risk. In 2024–2025 the portfolio's volatility was 9.8% against 15.5% for one wheel on average, and its worst drawdown −14.5% against −26.3%. The five wheels' monthly results barely moved together (median correlation 0.12).
  • The volatility premium showed up across stocks. In 2026, 36 of the 45 options sold were priced above the volatility that followed, by 6.8 points on average. In 2024–2025 a few large misses pulled the average gap to −1.1 points.
  • Holding won on return in 2026, +28.4% against +14.0%, at about the same Sharpe ratio. Once the wheel owns a stock, the call it sells caps the gain: APA returned +96.8% held, but +33.4% in its wheel.

Limits

  • No real crash in the test. Selling volatility means many small wins and a rare large loss. Mastercard's worst drop in 2026 was −18.4%, so about ten months of one stock does not show how the wheel would handle a crash.
  • Choosing the setting did not help. The chosen setting's 2026 Sharpe ratio was 2.55, against 2.59 for the default fixed before looking at any data. The result depends much more on the stock and the period than on the setting.
  • The screen's edge was within chance. The screen's five stocks beat eight randomly drawn S&P 100 stocks on average single-stock wheel Sharpe ratio in 2026, at the default setting (1.58 against 1.05), but by a margin chance alone could produce (p = 0.22).
  • Prices and costs. Daily closing quotes, no commissions.
