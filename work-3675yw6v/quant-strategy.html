The strategy: four sleeves, one book

The four sleeves
  • Trend-following. Long or short futures on stock indexes, bonds, currencies and commodities, following each market's price trend.
  • Merger arbitrage. Buys announced takeover targets to earn the small gap to the offer price if the deal closes.
  • Put-writing. Sells cash-secured put options on four ETFs and collects the premium.
  • Crash hedge. Part of the put premium buys far out-of-the-money puts that pay off only in a large fall.
How it was chosen and built
  • Rules written first. 60+ pre-registered strategy and overlay tests; most were rejected.
  • Walk-forward backtests on three windows, with block-bootstrap Sharpe tests and Holm correction for multiple testing.
  • Bias checks caught a look-ahead bias that fully explained one variant's 1.15 Sharpe, a data field that leaked deal outcomes, a futures-roll bug, and stale option quotes (re-marked, Sharpe restated).
  • Live trading through the Interactive Brokers API: automated scheduling with guarded manual recovery.
  • Who did what. The code was written with an AI coding assistant (Claude Code). I directed it: I chose the strategy, set the rules and guardrails, and made the research and live-run decisions.

How to read it
  • Lower drawdown than SPY. Drawdowns were far smaller than SPY's in every window, including the 2008 crisis.
  • Not higher return. Returns were well below SPY's in every window, and growth was close to the benchmark's, except in 2023–25, when it was about 2.3 points a year lower.
  • Not ahead every time. In 2023–25 the benchmark had the higher Sharpe and the smaller drawdown.

The live run: Nov 2 – Dec 1, 2026, on a paper account

The same rules, plus one sleeve added mid-run (below), trading $50M of paper (simulated) capital for 21 sessions. Live numbers load with the page.

A few sessions of paper trading are noise: these numbers say more about whether the system runs than about the strategy. On Nov 4, after seeing its 2026 test results, I added a small option-selling sleeve (the wheel, about 10% of the account); it is not part of the 2005–25 backtest.

Limits

  • No significant edge. The backtest Sharpe edge over the benchmark is not statistically significant. "Competitive risk-adjusted returns (backtest)" is the fair summary.
  • One window reused. 2023–25 was looked at again after the first look, so it is a second look, not an untouched holdout.
  • Paper money, short run. No real money is at risk, simulated fills may differ from real ones, and 21 sessions can't confirm or reject a strategy.
