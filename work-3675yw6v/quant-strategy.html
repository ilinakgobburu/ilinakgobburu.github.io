Backtest and live paper-trading evaluation of a multi-asset strategy

This project develops a single portfolio with four components, selected from more than 60 strategy and overlay tests whose rules were specified before each test was run. The portfolio is traded on an Interactive Brokers paper (simulated) account from November 2 to December 1, 2026, and is evaluated against a benchmark of one-third SPY and two-thirds Treasury bills. In the backtests, its main advantage over SPY is a smaller drawdown rather than a higher return.

Strategy design

Components
  • Trend-following. Long and short positions in equity index, bond, currency and commodity futures, taken in the direction of each market's price trend.
  • Merger arbitrage. Long positions in the targets of announced acquisitions, which earn the spread to the offer price if the deal is completed.
  • Put-writing. Cash-secured short put options on four ETFs, which earn the option premium.
  • Crash hedge. Part of the put premium funds far out-of-the-money puts, which pay off only in a severe market decline.
Methodology
  • Pre-registration. More than 60 strategy and overlay tests were specified before they were run. Most were rejected.
  • Evaluation. Walk-forward backtests over three windows, with block-bootstrap tests of the Sharpe ratio and a Holm correction for multiple testing.
  • Execution. Orders are placed through the Interactive Brokers API on an automated schedule, with safeguards for manual recovery.

Interpretation
  • Drawdowns. The maximum drawdown was far smaller than SPY's in every window, including the 2008 financial crisis.
  • Returns. Returns were well below SPY's in every window. Growth was close to the benchmark's except in 2023–25, when it was about 2.3 percentage points a year lower.
  • Comparison with the benchmark. In 2023–25 the benchmark had both the higher Sharpe ratio and the smaller drawdown.

Live paper-trading evaluation, Nov 2 – Dec 1, 2026

The same rules, with one component added during the run (described below), are traded on $50M of paper (simulated) capital for 21 sessions. Live figures load with the page.

Over a few weeks, returns are dominated by noise, so these figures speak more to the reliability of the trading system than to the merit of the strategy. Two changes made during the run are not part of the 2005–25 backtest. On Nov 4, after reviewing its 2026 test results, I added a small option-selling component (the wheel, about 10% of the account). From the Nov 23 roll, I raised merger arbitrage to half of the non-trend allocation, above the backtest's 40% cap, although an earlier test had not supported this change.

Limitations

  • Statistical significance. The backtest Sharpe ratio's advantage over the benchmark is not statistically significant. The results support competitive risk-adjusted returns in backtests, not outperformance.
  • Reuse of the 2023–25 window. This window was examined more than once during development, so it is not an untouched holdout sample.
  • Paper trading over a short horizon. No capital is at risk, simulated fills may differ from actual executions, and 21 sessions are too few to confirm or reject the strategy.
