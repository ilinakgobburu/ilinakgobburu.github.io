A survivorship-bias-free S&P 500 panel, point in time since 1996

Every firm in the S&P 500 on each date since 1996, including the ones later removed, merged or delisted, with a permanent SEC ID (CIK) for firms from 2016 on, so renamed and reused tickers don't split one company or splice two together. Built from free sources and checked against CRSP's licensed list. First result: backtesting on today's member list overstates an equal-weighted S&P 500 by 5.1 points a year. A daily volatility layer from intraday trades is being added.

Why it is needed: free index lists only know the survivors

  • Today's list hides the losers. Free S&P 500 lists give only today's members. A backtest built on them quietly drops every firm that failed or was acquired, so the strategy looks better than it was.
  • Tickers are not firms. CCI was Citicorp in 1996 and is Crown Castle today; FB became META. A join on ticker either splices two companies together or loses one.
  • The free volatility source closed. The standard free library of daily realized volatility closed in 2022, and the closest current one covers only today's large caps, so it has the same survivor problem.

  • It shows up in returns, not volatility. Portfolio volatility moved by at most 0.3 points a year across the six versions.
  • Leaving is not failing. Of the 116 firms delisted by 2025, 106 were acquired and 10 failed.

What is built, and what is not yet

Built
  • Index membership, 1996 to 2026. Every stay kept, including firms that left; it matches CRSP's licensed list on all but 92 of 1.27 million stock-days.
  • Permanent firm IDs from 2016 on, with the evidence for each ID, or the reason it was withheld, stored next to it.
  • One documented SQLite database, rebuilt from the raw inputs in one step; over 200 automated tests pass.
  • Daily macro series from FRED.
In progress
  • Daily realized volatility from intraday trades: built for the days downloaded so far; the full download started on Nov 17.
  • A covariance network between firms: built for the days downloaded so far.
  • Wikipedia attention data: an 18-firm sample so far.
  • Firm IDs before 2016: those exits are listed but not resolved yet.

Next: forecasting tests against a standard HAR model, using attention, network position and other signals.

Firm IDs: an LLM proposes, two sources check

How one ID is decided
  • A local LLM names the company that traded under the ticker on its last day in the index. That is its only job.
  • SEC EDGAR supplies the ID. The CIK is the one EDGAR's records give for that name, never a number the model wrote.
  • Wikipedia is the second witness. Its record of index changes says which firm S&P removed that day. When it disagrees with the model, the model's answer is dropped and Wikipedia's name is searched instead.
  • Conflicts are withheld, not guessed. When the evidence disagrees, the ID stays empty and the reason is recorded.
Why the checks matter

The model was also asked for each CIK. None of its 275 was right, and it rated every one "high" confidence. It fits names to letters: TAP became Atmos Energy (it is Molson Coors).

An early version trusted an EDGAR name match alone. An audit found that 44 of its 144 IDs (31%) were the wrong firm, real companies whose names happen to fit the ticker. With the second source and rules for known traps, the current build finds 213 of the 275 IDs and withholds the rest.

Limits

  • Firm IDs start in 2016. Exits before 2016 are listed but unresolved, and 58 segments from 2016 on still have no ID.
  • Older tickers. The free membership file uses today's tickers for some older companies. The three cases since 2016 (Tyco, the old Chubb and Arconic) are fixed; older ones are still folded in.
  • Draft results. The backtest and CRSP figures come from the team's draft brief of 2026-11-17; the volatility results wait for the full download.
  • The hand check is small and was not blind. On a 30-stay random sample, 24 IDs agreed, none was wrong and 6 were blank. The answers were drafted by an AI assistant after seeing the pipeline's output and reviewed by a team member. With 30 rows, these are estimates.
  • Licensed data stays licensed. The membership and firm-ID layers use only public sources. The CRSP check and the backtest use licensed CRSP returns, and only aggregate results are shown; nothing from the licensed intraday data is published.
