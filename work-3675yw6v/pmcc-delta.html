Poor Man's Covered Call on Delta Air Lines

A covered call needs 100 shares. A Poor Man's Covered Call holds one long-dated call (a LEAP) in their place and sells a one-month call against it each month, so it costs far less to open, but the LEAP loses time value every day and shares do not. Delta was the test case because airline demand is seasonal, and this structure does best when one month is priced richly against the year as a whole.

How it was tested

  • Daily backtest with no look-ahead. Every decision uses only that day's quotes.
  • Black-Scholes valuation. Implied volatility and delta for each option come from its quoted price.
  • Reg T margin, every day. The account is checked against margin rules on each day of the test, including nights after an assignment.
  • Same engine, same data for the covered call and buy-and-hold benchmarks, plus a stress test at bid and ask prices.
  • 36 tests on the engine, checked against answers worked out by hand.

Results

The strategy made money over a period in which holding the stock lost it, and it needed far less cash to open. It did not survive worse fills: at bid and ask prices it lost $170.

Limits

  • One stock, one period. A single stock over one five-year window.
  • Prices. Daily closing quotes rather than guaranteed execution prices; the profit depends on filling near the mid.
  • Costs left out. No commissions, and no interest on cash.
