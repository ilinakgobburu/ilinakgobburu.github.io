Tracking tech's ripple effect on the market

NVIDIA lost 23% of its value in the five sessions of November 16–20, 2026, and the technology sector fund (XLK) fell 12.5% in price. This note measures what the other ten S&P 500 sectors did that week and how much of it tech's move explains. It looks at prices (each sector's beta to tech), money moving in and out of sector funds (ETF creations and redemptions) and option prices (implied volatility). It ends with Monday, Nov 23, when NVIDIA rose 31.5% and closed above where the week began.

How it was measured

  • Beta to tech. The slope of each sector fund's daily return on XLK's over the two years before the selloff. A beta of 0.30 means the sector moved 0.30% for every 1% tech moved. Like an option's greeks, gamma measures how that beta changes on big tech moves, and vega how the sector moves with tech's implied volatility.
  • ETF flows. The change in each fund's shares outstanding times its NAV. Shares are created when money comes in and redeemed when it leaves. Counts post about a day late, so flows are dated both ways, by trade date and by the date they posted. Neither view was chosen in advance, and the figures on this page are by trade date.
  • Implied volatility. Each fund's 30-day at-the-money implied vol, compared with the rise its usual link to tech's implied vol predicted.
  • Written down first. The funds, the baseline and the beta and flow measures were set before any data was pulled. The greeks, the three-month beta tracker and the implied-vol data were added after the first price and share pull, before any of them was computed. Checks added after seeing the results are labeled as such.

Weeks are sums of the five daily log returns, which differ a little from price changes (XLK's week was −13.3 by this measure). Flows are by trade date, Monday to Thursday, because Friday's had not posted for 13 of the 15 funds.

  • The extra losses came before Friday. On Friday, when XLK fell 7.3%, every sector landed within 0.3 points of its prediction or fell less. The gap built up mostly Tuesday to Thursday, on days tech moved −0.9%, +0.3% and −1.9%. (This day-by-day split was added after seeing the results.)
  • Beta had already faded. The two-year beta includes the April 2025 tariff crash, when everything moved together. Over the three months before the selloff, most sectors had little or no beta to tech, so on that measure the misses are even bigger.
  • No rotation into defensive sectors. Through Thursday, health care lost $912m and staples $154m, while utilities and real estate took in only $37m and $30m. (By the date posted, staples took in $230m.) Energy and gold each lost about $1.4bn. Long Treasuries took in $959m, more than usual (89th percentile), and T-bills $2.06bn, about normal for a fund that grows most weeks. Communication services took in $319m, most of it on Wednesday.
  • Implied vol rose in every sector. Real estate's rose 18 points against about 3 predicted, though its series is one of the three noisy ones (see Limits). Financials' rose 12.8 points against 8.9. Energy and consumer discretionary rose less than predicted.
  • Adding tech's implied vol narrows the gap. Adding it to the prediction (an after-the-fact check) closed 20–48% of the week's gap for the sectors that fell more than predicted, but it overshot Friday's drop for every sector by 0.4 to 1.8 points and moved consumer discretionary's prediction too low. On Monday, with the fit fixed in advance, it did worse than beta alone.

The rebound on Monday, Nov 23

NVIDIA's price rose 31.5% on Monday and closed 1.4% above its Nov 13 close. Its beta to tech accounts for about 11 of its 27 log points. The rest was specific to NVIDIA, a miss of 9.5 standard deviations. XLK rose 9.0% and made back about two-thirds of the week's loss. It still closed 4.6% below its Nov 13 close.

  • Other sectors moved with their betas. Nine of the ten landed within 0.8 standard deviations of their beta prediction. In daily log returns, industrials rose 3.83% against 3.86% predicted and SPY 4.60% against 4.58%. Energy fell 1.06% where beta predicted a 1.47% rise (−1.7 standard deviations).
  • The week's extra losses stayed. Because Monday's moves matched beta, the losses beyond beta from Nov 16–20 were still there at Monday's close. Every sector fund was still down over the six sessions. Eight sectors made back 19% (health care) to 60% (consumer discretionary) of the week's loss, and staples and energy fell further.
  • Adding vega did not help. The beta + vega prediction, added after seeing the week's results, was fixed before Monday's data. It was closer for five sectors and further off for five, and its average miss (0.75 points) was larger than beta's alone (0.64).
  • Implied vol came down with tech's. XLK's 30-day at-the-money implied vol fell from 47.6 to 38.4 and the S&P 500's from 29.4 to 21.9. Six of the eight series that are not noisy fell within 1.5 points of their vol-beta prediction.

Monday's prices and implied vol were pulled on Nov 24, and the measures for this section were written down before that pull. Every prediction uses the two-year coefficients, not refitted.

Limits

  • One week is one event. These data show what moved, not why. NVIDIA reported earnings on Nov 17, and its biggest drop came three days later.
  • ETF flows are only part of the money. Mutual funds, single stocks and futures aren't seen, some creations are arbitrage rather than investors, and flows can't be traced from one fund to another.
  • Friday's and Monday's flows are missing. By the second pull on Nov 24, Friday's trades had still not posted for 13 of the 15 funds, so they are left out rather than counted as zero. Monday's are not shown either, because share counts post a day or more late.
  • Noisy option series. The implied vol series for materials, utilities and real estate jump around, so their changes should be read with care.
