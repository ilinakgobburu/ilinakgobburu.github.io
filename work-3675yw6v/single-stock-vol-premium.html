A decade of single-stock option premium, erased by one month

Is a one-month at-the-money option on a typical S&P 500 stock priced above the volatility the stock then delivers, and by how much? Each month, take the median stock (about 460 stocks, 2014–2023) and compare its implied vol with the next 21 trading days' realized vol: in vol points, and in variance, the unit a delta-hedged option seller's P&L is actually built on. The rules were registered before anything was computed.

What it found

  • Usually a small premium. In the median month, the typical stock's options were priced about 2.7 vol points above what followed, roughly a tenth of implied vol.
  • Zero in the seller's own units. In variance, the decade's average was about zero, because March 2020 wiped out the rest. Leaving that month out brings back a clear premium, but a seller can't leave out the crash.
  • The classic short-volatility shape. The average good month earned +4.1 vol points; the average bad month lost −5.8, and a few were very large.
  • Shocks start calm. The first month of every shock in the decade (August 2015, February 2018, October 2018, February 2020) opened with options looking rich. The gauge turned negative only after each crash, just as selling became profitable again.

How it was tested

  • Registered first. The question, the measures and the stress windows were committed before anything was computed, and the study was run once.
  • The typical company, not the index. The median stock each month, so the largest tech names don't dominate.
  • Honest error bars. Standard errors (with Newey-West) come from the 120 monthly numbers, not from 55,000 stock-months that all move with the market.
  • Checked by hand and for look-ahead. Worked examples recomputed by hand matched the run exactly, and tests check that nothing after the formation date can move the gauge.

Limits

  • Not a trading P&L. No spreads or hedging costs; a seller earns less than this premium.
  • Survivorship. The stocks are the index as of the end of 2023.
  • One option per stock. One-month, at-the-money only; earnings timing is approximated by the calendar.
