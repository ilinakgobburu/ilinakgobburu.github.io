Which stocks' options are most overpriced? A one-line rule beat the ML

Each month, rank S&P 500 stocks by how overpriced their one-month at-the-money options look, then check against the volatility the stock actually delivered. Four contenders, from simple to complex: sell everything, a one-line ratio rule, logistic regression and a shallow random forest. The models, the scoring, the pass bar and the rule for which model goes to the final test were all registered before any feature, label or model fit was computed. The final test was then run once.

"Sell everything" scores zero by construction. The forest edged the rule in the final test, but by noise, and it wasn't the pre-chosen model, so it can't be claimed.

How it was tested

  • Registered first. The question, the contenders, the scoring, the pass bar and the choice rule were committed before any code, feature or model fit. The underlying stock panel had been built earlier, for a related study.
  • Walk-forward. An expanding window refit every January, with any row whose outcome wasn't yet known purged from training.
  • Reviewed before the run. An independent review added tests for possible look-ahead paths before the development run.
  • One shot at the final test. The development result was recomputed exactly first, then 2021–23 was run once.
  • Honest error bars. t-statistics come from the monthly results, not from tens of thousands of stock-months that all move with the market.

Limits

  • Relative, not profit. "Richer than the average stock" is a ranking result, not a trading P&L.
  • Fading edge. The simple rule scored about half its development size in the final test, and only +0.35 in 2023.
  • Survivorship. The stocks are the S&P 500 as of the end of 2023; none of nine departed firms checked had implied vol in the vendor's data.
  • Weak classifiers. As yes/no predictors the models were weak (AUC 0.50–0.52 in the final test); any value is in the ranking's top fifth.
