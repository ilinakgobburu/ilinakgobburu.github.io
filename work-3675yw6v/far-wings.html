Selling the far wings, held to expiry

A nightly options algorithm for the course's paper-trading server. Once a month it sells one far out-of-the-money put and one far out-of-the-money call on each stock and holds them to expiry, with no hedge. The registered version, written down before the final test, hedged with shares and did not pass. The hedge was removed after that result. The unhedged re-run passes, but because of that change it is not a clean out-of-sample result.

How it was tested

  • Why the far wings. Two exploratory studies of the development option chains found that a median 63% of the put side's extra implied variance sat in puts under 0.10 delta. They found the far calls priced up too. Sold delta-hedged, the far wings earned the most before costs, but after costs no delta zone clearly paid. These studies chose the strategy and are not evidence that it works.
  • The rules. Each month, on each stock, sell the put nearest 0.05 delta and the call nearest 0.03 delta and hold both to expiry. Each wing is sized at about $200 of Black-76 vega on a $100,000 book.
  • Two stages. Development ran January 2019 to December 2023 on five stocks (NVDA, AMD, DIS, COST, DAL). The final test ran January 2024 to October 2026 on six (NVDA, MA, V, APA, ADI, FISV).
  • Three fills. The course server fills orders only in the next session. So the headline limit fill sells at the entry close's mid, halfway between bid and ask, only if the next session's closing mid is at least that price. Otherwise the wing sits out. The stress fill always sells at the bid. A third run sells every wing at the mid with no costs, as a reference that was not judged against the bar.
  • Written down first. Each study was registered before its code ran on real data. The registration set the pass bar (Sharpe ratio at least 0.5 at the limit fill and above 0 at the stress fill), a share hedge, and four crash cycles to check.

Figures are for each stage's pooled book, which combines one $100,000 book per stock, from the no-hedge re-run. They do not cover the live basket. Returns are monthly returns on a fixed book added up, not compounded, so a drawdown can exceed 100%. Volatility is the monthly standard deviation × √12, and Sharpe is the mean month × 12 divided by it.

  • Development lost money. At the limit fill 52 of 60 months were up and every year but 2020 gained. March 2020 alone lost 99.0% of the pooled book, and DAL's cycle entered in February 2020 lost 413.7% of its own book. This stage would not have passed the bar.
  • The no-hedge re-run passed the registered bar in the final test. The pooled Sharpe ratio was +2.74 at the limit fill (t-statistic +4.61, 34 months) and +0.30 at the stress fill (t-statistic +0.50). Leaving out any one of the four crash cycles named before the test keeps it at +2.71 or more at the limit fill and +0.29 or more at the stress fill.
  • The headline depends on which orders did not fill. In the final test the limit fill sold 84 puts and 77 calls over 198 stock-cycles (one stock in one monthly cycle), against 198 and 161 at the stress fill. It left wings unsold in all four named crash cycles. In the cycle entered October 20, 2025, which held FISV's −44% day, the limit fill never sold FISV's put and FISV's cycle made $1,678. The stress fill sold it and FISV's cycle lost $132,669, 132.7% of its own book.
  • In development the limit fill did slightly worse. Its Sharpe ratio was −0.18, against −0.16 at the stress fill, which pays the spread. A limit at the mid fills only when the option's price has not fallen by the next close, so it skipped the wings already moving the seller's way. Only 121 of 295 puts and 106 of 295 calls filled, and they lost $185,609 on their first night.

The registered hedge and its removal

The version registered before the final test hedged with shares whenever the book's dollar delta moved outside a $5,000 band. It did not pass, with Sharpe ratios of −0.28 at the limit fill and −0.85 at the stress fill. After that result the hedge was removed and the registered runs were repeated with the band set to infinity. Every cycle's options matched the registered run's, so the hedge is the only difference. Apart from the hedged results in this section, all backtest figures on this page come from that re-run.

  • In the final test the hedge lost more than the options made. The options alone made $190,096 at the limit fill and $67,611 at the stress fill, while the hedge's shares lost $235,083 and $436,566. The hedge sells after drops and buys after rises, so it loses when moves reverse, as after the rally of April 9, 2025.
  • In 2020 the hedge saved the book. In development its shares made $366,200 at the stress fill. Without them March 2020 lost 96% of the pooled book. With them the worst month was −21.3%, in February 2020.

This split into options and shares was also made after the verdict and is not a test.

The live run on the course server

The live run trades the same rules, with no hedge, on NVDA and the 30 largest S&P 500 companies by market value on December 31, 2018. Each stock is its own $100,000 book. Of those 30, only Disney (DIS), Mastercard (MA) and Visa (V) were in either backtest, so 27 of the 31 live stocks were never backtested.

Each weeknight a script checks its settings and the clock, then writes the next session's orders. On the first session after a monthly expiry it places a limit sell for each wing at the closing mid, and it closes any assigned shares at the next open.

The first entry was a one-off, off the monthly schedule, chosen from the November 17, 2026 close for November 18, into the December 18 expiry. The script wrote 60 option orders, both wings on 29 stocks and the put alone on two that had no eligible call. The course server does not resolve orders yet, so there is no live result. The next entry will be chosen from the December 21, 2026 close.

Limits

  • Not a clean out-of-sample pass. The no-hedge version was chosen after the registered, hedged version failed, and it was judged on the same final-test data. It also would not have passed in development. The live run is its first test on new data.
  • A thin margin at the stress fill. The Sharpe ratio of +0.30 has a t-statistic of +0.50, and the stress fill lost 24.0% of the pooled book in 2025.
  • Unhedged crash risk. The strategy collects small premiums most months and can lose nearly the whole pooled book in one month, as March 2020 showed. Neither the no-hedge re-run nor the live run has a hedge or a cap on crash losses.
  • Exposure to the stocks' direction. With no hedge the book keeps the options' exposure to which way the stocks move (delta). In the final test the P&L split credits delta with +⁠$189,160 of the +⁠$190,096 made at the limit fill, and +⁠$335,002 at the stress fill, where the total was +⁠$67,611. So nearly all of the final-test profit came from how the six stocks moved. In development the same part lost $52,717 and $210,949.
  • Small samples. The final test covers six stocks over 34 months, and most of the live basket was never backtested.
  • Modeled fills. How the course server will fill the live limit orders is not known yet.
