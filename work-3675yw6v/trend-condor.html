The put-tilted trend condor on NVDA

An iron condor sells a put and a call and buys a farther put and call as protection, so its loss is capped. This one trades the weekly options of NVIDIA (NVDA), and only in weeks when they look expensive against NVDA's own recent volatility. The sold call sits farther out than the sold put. In the headline version, a trigger also hedges the direction away once NVDA moves far from the entry. The rules were registered before any of the strategy's results were computed. A second version without the trigger, called tilt only, was picked after seeing the test.

The rules

  • One condor a week. Decided at the close of the week's first session and held to that week's expiry. Each leg trades one contract for every 100 shares that $100,000 buys at the close.
  • A richness gate. The condor trades only when its edge is positive. The edge is the sold options' bid minus their fair value, plus the bought options' fair value minus their ask. Fair value is priced at NVDA's realized volatility over the last 21 sessions, so a positive edge means the options look expensive even after paying the spread.
  • The tilt. The sold put has at most a 20% chance of finishing in the money and the sold call at most 10%, so the call sits farther out. Of the condors that pass four risk limits, one of them a $10,000 maximum loss, it takes the one with the largest edge.
  • The trigger, headline only. Once NVDA closes more than one weekly standard deviation from the entry close, the book trades shares to remove its exposure to direction (delta-neutral). It stays neutral until expiry. The standard deviation uses the implied volatility at entry. Tilt only is the same strategy without the trigger.
  • The judged fill. This is the fill the bar is judged on. Every option trades at the entry close's bid or ask, and each share trade costs 5 basis points. Cash earns T-bill interest, and a short option is assigned only at the expiry close.

One $100,000 book each, 34 months. Sharpe ratios use monthly returns. The judged fill trades every option at the closing bid or ask. The next-day limit column places a limit order at the mid (halfway between bid and ask) for the next day, filled only if that day's mid reaches it. The mid column fills every option at the mid. Return above T-bills adds up the monthly returns less the T-bill, not compounded. The largest drop is from a peak with T-bill interest included, so it can be smaller than the worst month above T-bills (−2.83% for the headline, −5.37% for tilt only). The plain condor sells both options at up to a 20% chance of finishing in the money, and trigger only adds the trigger to it.

  • Registered and run once. The headline's 0.987 cleared the 0.5 bar set before the test (t-statistic 1.66). Its book grew 23.5% over the test, T-bill interest included, against 13.2% for cash in T-bills.
  • Tilt only was chosen after seeing this test, because it had the highest judged Sharpe of the condor books. So its 1.127 is not a test of it.
  • Without the tilt, the condor lost to T-bills. The plain condor scored −0.379 and trigger only −0.747.
  • The pass depends on the fill and assignment rules. Both tilted versions stayed positive at the mid but turned negative with a next-day limit order. The course server assigns a short option at any close where it is in the money. Under that rule, they scored −0.172 and −0.273.
  • Holding NVDA scored higher, 1.404, with a largest drop of −21.59% against −2.47% for the headline.
  • A screen came first. Each version had to pass a monthly screen on six stocks, using data already seen, to enter the weekly test. It needed a Sharpe above 0 in two windows, July 2024 to December 2025 and January to October 2026. The headline scored 0.940 and 2.234.

  • Time decay and gamma nearly cancel. The headline collected $50,518 of time decay and gave back $46,041 to gamma, the cost of NVDA's moves against the short options.
  • Vega lost money in all three. On balance, implied volatility rose while the condors were short it. With theta and gamma added, the volatility lines still lost money in every book.
  • Direction is where the tilted versions earned. Delta made $7,997 for the headline and $14,462 for tilt only, 79% and 85% of each one's P&L above the T-bill interest. The residual is also large in both. It is the part the daily greeks leave unexplained, so the share from direction counts delta alone.

This is not a pure volatility bet. It does not forecast direction, but it is designed around NVDA's upward drift. In the backtest, most of its profit came from how it handled direction.

The 52-week forward walk

  • Not started. The walk covers 52 weekly entries, starting with the first one after the code freeze. They are expected to run from Nov 30, 2026 to Nov 22, 2027, scoring December 2026 to November 2027. The code is not frozen yet, and 0 weeks are scored.
  • Registered first. The walk was registered on November 23, 2026, before any forward week was pulled or computed. The headline and tilt only each run on a fresh $100,000 book with the same code. One amendment the same day, also before any forward week, changed how partial months are scored.
  • The bar. Each version needs a pooled monthly Sharpe of at least 0.5 at the judged fill, the same bar and measure as the weekly test. Nothing changes once it starts, and there is no early stop.
  • What one year can say. With 12 monthly returns, a strategy with no edge still clears 0.5 about 3 times in 10, and one with a true Sharpe of 1.0 about 7 times in 10. The bar filters out clear losers but does not prove an edge.

Limits

  • One stock in a rising market. Most of the profit came from direction, in a period when NVDA itself returned +155.24% above T-bills (monthly returns summed, or +326.1% compounded with T-bill interest). The backtest says little about a flat or falling NVDA.
  • Historical weeks. The weekly test was registered and run once, but it covers 2024–26, the same years the six-stock screen used. Only the forward walk tests weeks that have not happened yet.
  • Fill and assignment assumptions. The judged fill assumes the trade is decided and filled near the close. Assigning short options only at expiry is an assumption, since NVDA's options are American-style and a holder can exercise earlier.
  • A large residual. $11,062 of the headline's P&L, more than its whole +⁠$10,090 above T-bill interest, is not explained by the daily greeks.
