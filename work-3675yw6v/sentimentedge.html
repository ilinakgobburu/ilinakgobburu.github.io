SentimentEdge, a news-sentiment paper-trading app

A five-person team built a web app that scores company news with two sentiment models, shows the scores next to prices, and lets users and four automated bots trade with simulated money. It was built over six sprints for a software engineering course. The demo below is the team's own frontend, running on data recorded from one local run of the app.

The demo

The demo is the team's React frontend from the final version of the code, rebuilt so it runs without a server. Each request a page makes is answered from responses recorded on November 27, 2026, from a local copy of the app with live news and prices. Charts, filters, ticker search and the two research papers all work. Placing trades and running new backtests are turned off, and nothing is sent anywhere.

The demo account's trades are sample trades, placed on past dates at real closing prices so its charts have a month of history. The bots started on the day of the recording. The demo is built for a laptop or desktop screen.

What the app does

  • Scores the news. Company news for 50 large stocks comes from the Finnhub API. Each article is scored by VADER, a rule-based model, and by FinBERT, a language model trained on financial text. The scores are combined per stock over several time windows.
  • Shows sentiment next to prices. The dashboard has the account, a sentiment heatmap and a news feed. Each stock's page shows its price history, its sentiment scores and the scored articles, and for some stocks a chart of features a language model extracted from the articles.
  • Trades with simulated money. Users buy and sell at live prices. Four bots trade once a day. Two follow the VADER or FinBERT scores, one follows the team's machine-learning model, and one buys and holds.
  • Checks the signals. A reports page measures how often sentiment called the next day's direction, with a Brier score and a calibration chart, and splits the account's profit and loss by news source.

The research track

The team first tried to predict each stock's next-day direction from its news sentiment. Over 16 tuned experiments the validation AUC rose from 0.55 to 0.65, but on held-out data it stayed near 0.5, which is no better than chance.

The team then changed the question to ranking the 50 stocks by their next 5-day return. With walk-forward validation over 10 folds, the final model reached a Spearman correlation of 0.083 between predicted and realized ranks, on 10,779 stock-days from April 2025 to March 2026. It averages five LightGBM models trained on 34 features. Features that a large language model extracted from the articles did not improve on the VADER and FinBERT scores.

These are development results. An automated experiment loop kept each change that raised the walk-forward score, so 0.083 is the score the model was tuned on and is likely optimistic.

The team worked in six sprints, with a review and a retrospective after each one. Changes were merged through 150 merge requests, and every commit on the main branch, apart from merges, follows the Conventional Commits format. The backend tests run with pytest on SQLite, and the frontend tests with Vitest. The app also has rate limiting, audit logging and Google sign-in.

Limitations

  • A recording, not the running app. The demo's prices, news and scores come from one run on November 27, 2026, and do not change. No language-model key was used for the recording, so the feature chart on the stock pages is empty.
  • Sample data in the demo account. Its trades were placed for the demo. The bots traded once, on the day of the recording. That day the app's volatility rule flagged a crash regime, which moves every bot into an equal-weight portfolio of all 50 stocks, so the VADER, FinBERT and buy-and-hold bots hold the same positions. The machine-learning bot holds cash because the prediction file it reads ends in March 2026.
  • A weak, optimistic signal. A rank correlation of 0.083 is small, it comes from about a year of news for 50 large stocks, and it is the score the model was tuned on.
  • Unfinished parts. The Reddit filter on the stock pages shows no articles, because collecting Reddit posts was never finished. The home page and the stock chart's legend describe a sentiment line over the price chart, but the final code draws only the price.
  • One correction in the demo. The app's research card for the ranking paper showed 0.1157, which is the score of a separate volatility model. The demo shows the ranking model's 0.0830.
