Tracking tech's ripple effect on the market

NVIDIA lost 23% of its value in the five sessions of November 16–20, 2026, and the technology sector fund (XLK) fell 12.5%. This note measures what the other ten S&P 500 sectors did that week and how much of it tech's move explains. It looks at prices (each sector's beta to tech), money moving in and out of sector funds (ETF creations and redemptions) and option prices (implied volatility).

How it was measured

  • Beta to tech. The slope of each sector fund's daily return on XLK's over the two years before the selloff. A beta of 0.30 means the sector moved 0.30% for every 1% tech moved. Like an option's greeks, gamma measures how that beta changes on big tech moves, and vega how the sector moves with tech's implied volatility.
  • ETF flows. The change in each fund's shares outstanding times its NAV. Shares are created when money comes in and redeemed when it leaves. Counts post about a day late, so flows are shown both by trade date and by the date they posted.
  • Implied volatility. Each fund's 30-day at-the-money implied vol, compared with the rise its usual link to tech's implied vol predicted.
  • Written down first. The funds, the baseline and the beta and flow measures were set before any data was pulled. Four checks added after seeing the results are labeled as such.

Weeks are sums of the five daily log returns. Flows are by trade date, Monday to Thursday, because Friday's had not posted.

  • The extra losses came before Friday. On Friday, when XLK fell 7.3%, every sector landed within 0.3 points of its prediction or fell less. The gap built up Tuesday to Thursday, on days tech moved −0.9%, +0.3% and −1.9%. (This day-by-day split was added after seeing the results.)
  • Beta had already faded. The two-year beta includes the April 2025 tariff crash, when everything moved together. Over the three months before the selloff, most sectors had little or no beta to tech, so on that measure the misses are even bigger.
  • No rotation into defensive sectors. Through Thursday, health care lost $912m and staples $154m, while utilities and real estate took in only $37m and $30m. Energy and gold each lost about $1.4bn. The money that came in went to long Treasuries ($959m) and T-bills ($2.06bn).
  • Implied vol rose in every sector. Real estate's rose 18 points against about 3 predicted. Energy and consumer discretionary rose less than predicted.
  • Tech's implied vol explains part of the gap. Adding it to the prediction (an after-the-fact check) closed 20–48% of the week's gap for the sectors that fell more than predicted, but it overshot Friday's drop for every sector.

Limits

  • One week is one event. These data show what moved, not why. NVIDIA reported earnings on Nov 17, and its biggest drop came three days later.
  • ETF flows are only part of the money. Mutual funds, single stocks and futures aren't seen, some creations are arbitrage rather than investors, and flows can't be traced from one fund to another.
  • Friday's flows are missing. By the second pull on Nov 24, Friday's trades had still not posted for 13 of the 15 funds, so they are left out rather than counted as zero.
  • Noisy option series. The implied vol series for materials, utilities and real estate jump around, so their changes should be read with care.
